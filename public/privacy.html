Privacy policy

Short version: MusicNuke has no database and no analytics. It keeps nothing about you on its server.

What MusicNuke can see

When you log in with Spotify, you give MusicNuke permission to:

  • Read your Spotify display name, user ID and profile picture.
  • Read your playlists (including private and collaborative ones) and your Liked Songs.
  • Edit those playlists and your Liked Songs, which is how it removes songs and puts them back when you hit Undo.

It uses this only to show you your playlists, find the songs you ask it to find, and remove or restore the songs you choose. It never sees your Spotify password or email address.

What is stored, and where

  • A login cookie (sn_auth) in your browser. It holds the Spotify access and refresh tokens, when they expire, your Spotify user ID and, if you log in through your own Spotify app, its Client ID. It is encrypted so only MusicNuke's server can read it. It expires after 30 days, and logging out deletes it.
  • A short-lived login cookie (sn_state) that protects the login process. It lasts up to 10 minutes and is deleted as soon as you finish logging in.
  • Your browser's local storage keeps your Client ID (if you use your own Spotify app) and which login button you used last, so logging in next time is one click.
  • Undo information is kept only in the open page and is gone when you leave or refresh it.

Your playlists and songs pass through MusicNuke's server only while it handles your request. Nothing is saved there.

What MusicNuke doesn't do

  • No analytics, tracking or advertising.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of your information.
  • No third-party or tracking cookies.

Other services involved

  • Spotify handles your login and provides all music data. Album and artist images load straight from Spotify's servers. Spotify's own privacy policy applies to your Spotify account.
  • Vercel hosts MusicNuke. Like any web host, it keeps basic request logs (such as IP addresses and the pages requested) for a limited time. If something goes wrong, a short error message may be written to these logs, but never your tokens or playlist contents.

Cookies

MusicNuke only uses the two login cookies above. They are needed for the site to work, so there is no cookie banner. You can delete them at any time in your browser settings, which logs you out. If you block cookies, you won't be able to log in.

Your choices

  • Log out to delete the login cookie.
  • Remove MusicNuke's access to your Spotify account at any time from Spotify's Manage apps page. If you used your own Spotify app, you can delete it in the Spotify Developer Dashboard.
  • Clear this site's data in your browser to remove the saved Client ID.

Because nothing is kept on the server, there is nothing else to delete.
